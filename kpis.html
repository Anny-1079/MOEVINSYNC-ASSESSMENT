Question 2

How I would judge the Facilities Team

A good team gives people a desk when they want one, does not pay for space nobody uses, and keeps the building safe, clean and pleasant. No single number shows all three, so each space metric is read next to an experience metric, per office, every week.

Reading occupancy and satisfaction together

Occupancy alone can look like success when it is actually crowding, or like failure when people simply like the space.

High occupancy, high satisfaction

Working well

Watch headroom. If occupancy stays above 95%, add day caps before it turns into denials.

High occupancy, low satisfaction

Crowded

Look at noise, temperature, desk quality and denial rate on the busy days. Tighten caps or add peak-day seats.

Low occupancy, high satisfaction

Comfortable but costly

Release a floor, sublet, or merge zones. People are happy, the money is not.

Low occupancy, low satisfaction

Wrong space or wrong reasons to come

Ask why people stay away. Location, tools, food and team presence usually explain it.

Rules I would act on

Peak occupancy under 70% for 8 weeks

Release floors or sublet. The plan is too big for how people really come in.

Peak occupancy above 92% for 3 weeks

Tighten caps and anchor days first. Add seats only if the denial rate stays above 3%.

Check-in rate under 85%

Shorten the time before an unchecked desk is released back to the pool.

Satisfaction down while occupancy is up

Fix comfort and noise on the busy days before changing capacity.

Reporting rhythm

  • Weekly: occupancy, denials, check-in, tickets and cleaning scores per office, shared with site leads.
  • Monthly: cost, adoption, meeting rooms, meal wastage and safety, reviewed with the facilities head.
  • Quarterly: satisfaction survey and a right-sizing review of the whole estate.
