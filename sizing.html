Question 1

How many seats are needed

Between 1,200 and 2,000, with 1,500 as the working number. Here is how each figure is reached and what moves it.

Minimum, maximum and the number I would plan for

Minimum 1,200 2,000 × 3 ÷ 5 = 1,200

Total office-days a week are 6,000. Spread over 5 days that is 1,200 a day. No plan can go lower, and this only works when Monday looks like Wednesday.

Recommended 1,500 1,200 × 1.25 = 1,500

People gravitate to Tuesday to Thursday for team meetings and manager calendars. I plan for a busiest day 25% above average. A reasonable range is 1,440 to 1,560.

Maximum 2,000 peak factor 5 ÷ 3 = 1.67

If every person picks the same three days, those days need a seat for everyone and the other two need none. Seats cannot exceed headcount.

Why not just build for 1,200?

The average day is not the day that matters. If 1,350 people arrive on a Wednesday and only 1,200 desks exist, 150 people have no place to work, and that is what employees remember. Leave and travel remove roughly 8 to 12% of attendance on a normal day, so I keep that as headroom for peak days and for growth.

How much the peak factor matters

Every step of 0.05 in the factor costs 60 seats. This is the one assumption I would test first with real data, so I show it plainly.

Three countries, three calendars

  • Size each site on its own peak, then add them up. Time zones and local holidays mean spare desks in one office cannot cover a shortage in another. The sum is higher than one pooled calculation.
  • Check the weekend. A site whose weekend is Friday and Saturday has a different working week. The 3-in-5 ratio becomes 3-in-5 on different days.
  • Match local rules. Fire and occupancy limits are set per building and per country. The seat count must stay under each limit on the busiest day.
  • Segment by role. Teams that need fixed equipment, such as labs or secure desks, are counted as fixed seats. The shared pool covers everyone else.

Beyond desks

Everything else in the building is sized to the busiest day of about 1,500 people plus visitors, not to the 2,000 on the payroll.

What would move the number

Pushes toward 2,000

  • A fixed "Tuesday to Thursday" rule
  • Leadership asking everyone in on the same day
  • Headcount growth or more client-facing roles
  • Poor commute options on other days

Pushes toward 1,200

  • Team-specific anchor days, staggered across the week
  • Per-day booking caps at about 90% of desks
  • Releasing unclaimed desks after a set time
  • More roles that are mostly remote
